## Goal

Produce an owner-reviewable specification of the smallest safe delivery lifecycle so contracts and controller tests can be implemented without inventing approval, retry or review-budget semantics in code.

## Constraints and non-goals

Vendor-neutral semantics. Preserve source-decided 2+1 bounds and human authority. Do not introduce vendor adapters, install dependencies, initialise external services, implement a scheduler or grant deployment permissions. Do not resolve policy questions by accidental defaults.

## Risk, design and quality

Route: Design. High impact because faulty semantics could allow duplicate writers, stale acceptance or unauthorised execution. No UI change; mockups and interactive UX prototypes are N/A. State/sequence tables and failure walkthroughs are the appropriate design evidence. Executable recovery experiments may be proposed as later spikes, not claimed complete here.

Proposed quality profile: Markdown/reference checks, independent scenario walkthrough, standard review then adversarial review and explicit human design approval. No executable controller exists, so runtime/unit/integration/performance checks are N/A for this design artifact; security/permission and recovery requirements must still be reviewed in scenarios.

Release impact: documentation/specification only; no deployable artifact or migration. The accepted specification will govern later implementation, so material edits require renewed approval. Outcome signal: subsequent contract implementation can map requirements to expected transitions without unresolved blocking semantics; owner reviews that during SF-002.
