Why a factory?

Coding agents can write a change in a minute. The hard part is knowing the change is right, without reading every line of every attempt yourself.

The factory puts your attention where it counts. Up front, you write a clear ticket. At the end, you look at a change that has already passed the checks and the reviews, and you decide.

Everything in between is run by a controller: a plain program, not another AI. It moves the work along, runs the checks itself, and stops to ask you whenever something does not add up.

The first goal

One READY ticket reaches a pull request you can review, with no one pushing it from stage to stage, and with a safe way to fail.

From the architecture baseline. Plan step 7 reached it on 2026-10-16. Since then the stages became data you can change, and the factory gained extensions.

Your workflow; the factory's rules

You choose the stages, their order, their budgets and where a human decides (D-023). These rules hold for every workflow. Each one has a room that shows it.

1 · The controller decides; agents claim

"Done, here is commit abc123" is a claim, not a fact. Controller and owner

2 · Evidence binds to one SHA

Check output and verdicts name the exact commit they are about. Checks and command stages

3 · Every loop spends a budget

A workflow with a way back must count each pass round it, and stops at the limit. Workflows

4 · Unclear means stop and ask

A crash, a timeout, a reply with no claim: the cycle is escalated to you. Workflows

5 · Candidate code is untrusted

Developer runs, checks and reviewers run in a sandbox. Sandbox and secrets

6 · Only you hand out a credential

A stage that acts outward needs a grant from your own files, and runs only on a reviewed commit. Sandbox and secrets
