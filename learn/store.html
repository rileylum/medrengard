A ledger, not a record

The factory never stores "the cycle is in STANDARD_REVIEW". It stores what happened: a claim, a result, a verification, a verdict. One event per row, in order, and no row is ever changed or deleted.

To know where a cycle is, the store reads its rows and replays them through apply, under the workflow the cycle was opened with. The state is a sum, like a bank balance worked out from the statement.

Why this way

  • A crash loses nothing: the rows are the truth, and a restart replays them.
  • Every decision has its reason on record: which event, from which stage, and apply's note.
  • Anyone can read what happened without asking the controller: that is the observation stream, below.

Try it: read a day's log

This runs directory holds a day of work on three work items. Read it the way events does: pick a work item or the whole directory, skip what you have seen with --after, or turn on --follow and let the rest of the day arrive. Click a line to read it.

seq is the row's place in the whole store, so lines from different work items interleave in the order they were written. from and stage are not stored: the reader works them out by replaying each cycle.

The observation stream

node src/main.ts events prints one JSON line per row. Each line gives the row's position, the time it was written, the cycle and work it belongs to, the event as stored, and the stage it moved the cycle from and to. With --follow it keeps reading: SQLite cannot notify another process, so it looks for new rows every 500 ms.

A reader decides nothing, and writes nothing except once, to number the rows of a store written before positions existed. It replays each cycle under the workflow its first row recorded, after checking that workflow's hash. A row it cannot replay, such as an event type from a newer controller, is passed on as stored with null stages, never escalated.

Observers

An observer is any process that reads the stream and acts. It may act only through your two commands, accept and cancel, which record its name with --source. The controller never starts one, and nothing it does is trusted more than you typing the command (D-024).

The kit for an observer (observerKit in src/kits/observe.ts) feeds it lines from a scratch store and checks only what it does through those two commands, including that an unknown event type makes it neither throw nor act. The example in tests/fixtures/comment-observer.ts accepts a change whose every changed line is blank or a comment.
